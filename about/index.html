About DeskLark Studio

Who publishes this site, what it actually is, and what it's for -- including an honest note about a few older pages that no longer match that focus.

Who publishes this site

guides.desklark.com is published by DeskLark Studio, a small independent studio. DeskLark Studio also publishes a handful of free budgeting calculators (linked in the footer of every page on this site) and a small line of budgeting printables sold through its Etsy shop. This site is not a bank, a financial institution, a law firm, an accounting firm, or a news organization, and it is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or reviewed by any government agency, app platform, or professional body.

This site's focus, in one sentence

This site's primary focus is personal and household budgeting for everyday money decisions -- the kind of planning that goes with a wedding, a move, a holiday season, a freelance side income, or an ordinary monthly budget.

What that looks like in practice

Most of what's here are short, practical guides that go with our free calculators, plus companion guides for the printables we sell -- covering things like cash stuffing and sinking funds, wedding budgeting, freelancer tax set-asides, debt payoff, and seasonal and life-event budgeting (moving, holidays, a new baby, a first apartment). You can see everything in one place on the all-guides page.

A note on the older business and compliance articles

A small number of older articles on this site are not about personal budgeting. This site also carries a few pages on B2B SaaS finance metrics (customer acquisition cost payback, net revenue retention, e-commerce and Airbnb profit margins) and a cluster of pages on U.S. state app-store age-verification laws for app developers. Those pages are still here -- we haven't taken them down, and we're not pretending they were never published -- but they are no longer this site's focus, and we are not adding to either topic going forward. If you arrived here from one of those pages, the rest of this site is about a different subject: budgeting for individuals and households, not business metrics or software compliance law.

What we don't claim

We don't have on-staff CPAs, attorneys, or financial planners, and no named individual reviews these guides for professional credential. Where a topic genuinely requires professional judgment specific to your situation -- a tax filing decision, a legal compliance question -- the guide says so and points you to a licensed professional or the primary government source, rather than presenting itself as that professional's opinion. Nothing on this site is personalized financial, tax, or legal advice.
