What Happens If You Don't Disclose AI Use on Etsy?

Etsy AI disclosure guide · DeskLark Studio

Search this question and you'll find seller blogs citing specific removal counts, warning totals, and exact enforcement start dates. We looked for a primary source for those numbers and couldn't find one -- so rather than repeat them, here's what Etsy's own policy pages actually say, and an honest note about where the scarier-sounding numbers seem to come from.

The rule itself, in Etsy's own words

"Sellers must disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI."

Source: Etsy's Creativity Standards ("Etsy's House Rules"), last updated Jun 10, 2025, cross-confirmed by the Etsy Seller Handbook article on AI creations, linked directly from the Creativity Standards page.

That's the entire rule, in the exact words Etsy uses. Not disclosing, when a listing was made using AI tools based on your own prompts, means the listing doesn't comply with a policy Etsy states plainly and currently maintains.

What we could confirm about enforcement

Etsy operates content moderation for its marketplace -- automated detection, seller reports, and manual review are all documented, unremarkable features of any large marketplace, and Etsy's own seller community forums contain ordinary seller discussion of listings being flagged or reviewed. That much is uncontroversial. What we could not find, anywhere in Etsy's own newsroom, press materials, or policy pages, or in mainstream trade press, was a specific, sourced figure for how many AI-disclosure-related listings have been removed, how many warnings have been issued, or an exact date enforcement "began."

A note about the specific numbers you may have seen elsewhere

Several seller-facing blog posts cite precise figures -- specific removal counts and warning counts tied to a specific enforcement start date -- when discussing this topic. We looked for the original source of those figures and could not find one: no Etsy statement, no trade-press reporting, no independent confirmation anywhere we checked. The same figures appear, worded almost identically, across a cluster of similar posts, several of which are also selling their own AI-compliance tools to Etsy sellers -- which is worth knowing, since it's a direct incentive to make the risk sound as large and urgent as possible.

None of that means the disclosure rule isn't real or isn't worth following -- it plainly is, in Etsy's own words above. It just means a specific statistic about how many listings have been removed isn't something we can verify, so we're not going to repeat it as fact. If you come across a number like this elsewhere, treat it as unverified until you can trace it back to Etsy itself or a credible, named news outlet, and check Etsy's current policy pages directly rather than taking a blog post's summary of them at face value.

What's actually at stake, stated plainly

A non-compliant listing risks whatever consequence Etsy's own moderation process applies to a policy violation on its platform -- Etsy alone decides what that is in any individual case, and no outside guide can promise a specific outcome either way. The reliable move isn't calculating your odds against an unverifiable statistic; it's writing an honest, accurate disclosure sentence for any listing AI genuinely touched, so the question of enforcement odds doesn't come up in the first place.

Disclosure isn't only a compliance question -- it's also a trust question

It's worth separating two different reasons to disclose. One is the policy requirement itself, covered above. The other is independent of Etsy's enforcement entirely: buyers on a handmade-and- creative marketplace often have real opinions about AI involvement, in either direction -- some actively seek it out, some actively avoid it, and plenty don't care either way as long as they know. An accurate disclosure sentence lets a buyer make that call for themselves before purchasing, rather than after, which tends to reduce dissatisfied buyers and post-purchase disputes regardless of what Etsy's enforcement actually looks like in practice. A well-written disclosure is closer to good-faith product information than a compliance chore.

If you already have undisclosed listings, here's a reasonable first step

Rather than trying to remember which of your listings involved AI and which didn't, work through your shop systematically: for each live listing, ask whether AI touched the image, the mockup, the design file, or the written description, based on your own prompts or inputs. Any listing where the answer is yes belongs in the "Designed by a seller" category and needs an accurate disclosure sentence added to its description. This is a one-time audit, not a recurring task -- once every current listing is sorted and disclosed, the ongoing work is just applying the same habit to new listings as you create them.

The practical fix takes a few minutes

For most sellers, closing the gap between "AI touched this listing" and "the listing discloses it" is a short task, not a project: identify which listings had AI involved in the image, the mockup, the design, or the written description, then add an accurate sentence to each one's description.
